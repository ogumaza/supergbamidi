This release adds support for Quintet's and Nintendo R&D2's sound drivers, and for Nintendo's
MusicPlayer2000 (MP2K), the sound driver in most GBA games. supergbamidi converts music from Game
Boy Advance games that use these drivers, or Konami's or Rare's, to MIDI and SoundFont. It finds
the driver in a ROM or GSF rip and plays each song through a model of that driver. Each song gets a
Standard MIDI File and a SoundFont built from the game's samples and instruments. For Konami's,
Quintet's and Nintendo R&D2's drivers and MP2K, the SoundFonts also include Game Boy PSG waveforms.

Requires 64-bit Windows 10 or later, with no additional runtime DLLs. Drop one or more `.gba`,
`.gsflib` or `.minigsf` files on `supergbamidi.exe`, or run it in a terminal; `supergbamidi --help`
lists the options. Each file's output goes in a folder next to it.

For macOS and Linux, follow the build instructions in the README. On macOS the build also makes
`Supergbamidi.app`, which you can drop files on in the Finder.

## Changes since 1.1.0

- Quintet's and Nintendo R&D2's drivers and MP2K are supported, in the games listed under
  "Features".
- `--driver quintet`, `--driver rd2` and `--driver mp2k` read a game with Quintet's driver,
  Nintendo R&D2's or MP2K, and `--info` names the driver when it finds it. With `--driver mp2k`,
  `--song-table` and `--song-count` read a song table that detection doesn't find, and with
  `--driver rd2`, `--song-table` gives the address of the game's settings for the driver.
- A game can have two drivers. A conversion uses the first that detection finds, `--driver` picks
  the other, and `--info` lists both.
- `--tracks` picks Quintet's channels by number: 0-3 are the PSG channels, and 4 and 5 the PCM
  channels.
- `--trace` lists the Quintet model's writes to the sound, DMA and timer registers. For MP2K it
  lists each active sound channel's state; for Nintendo R&D2's driver it lists each active
  voice's state and the PSG register writes.
- With Rare's driver, the loop markers start where every track is looping and every track without
  a loop has finished, and the loop lasts until every track is back where its loop started, up to
  8 times the longest track's loop. A tune no longer runs on through a track's closing waits, and
  a conversion that would run past an hour stops there, with a warning. The new drivers plan their
  loops the same way.
- With Konami's driver, a song's loop starts at the first of its tracks' loop points, since the
  driver sends every track back at once. A player that looped at the markers used to leave out the
  notes on the frames before the last track reached its loop point.
- A ZIP, 7-Zip, RAR or gzip file gets an error that says to extract the ROM or GSF files from it
  first.

## Features

- **Games.** Quintet's driver in *Super Robot Taisen A*, *Super Robot Taisen D*, *Super Robot
  Taisen R* and *Super Robot Taisen J*, which has a later revision of the driver, Nintendo R&D2's
  driver in *The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past*, and MP2K in *Pokémon Emerald* and in the game
  selection and *Four Swords* of *A Link to the Past & Four Swords*. Detection identifies each
  driver by its code, so other games with these revisions should work too.
- **MIDI.** With Quintet's driver, each MIDI file uses 37 MIDI ticks per song tick. Each note stays
  on its own tick within the frame the driver plays it in. Volume, pan and the master volume become
  CC11 and CC10, and detune, slides, the pitch envelope, the LFO and the frequency sweep become
  pitch bends, a frame at a time. With MP2K, each MIDI file keeps the song's 24 ticks to a quarter
  note, with the tempo slowed to match the GBA's frame rate, and follows the driver's choice of
  channels: it leaves out the notes that the driver drops or that never sound, and ends a note where
  a later note takes its channel. Volume and pan become CC7 and CC10, the reverb becomes the reverb
  send, and bends, tuning, the key shift and the vibrato become pitch bends. `--voice-channels`
  gives each of the driver's sound channels a MIDI channel instead of each track, so that a note
  stops where the game cuts it off, though a track's notes then move between channels. With Nintendo
  R&D2's driver, each MIDI file has 3600 ticks to a quarter note, one for each of the 150ths of a
  tick the driver counts. Each note stays on its own tick. Notes that the driver can't find a voice
  for are left out. Pan and the volumes become CC10 and CC11, and slides, bends and the LFO become
  pitch bends, a frame at a time.
- **SoundFont.** Quintet's square and wave channels get a sample for each duty and wave pattern,
  and an instrument that switches them during a note switches samples too. Noise drums are
  rendered from what the driver writes to the noise channel, with the Game Boy's LFSR, envelope and
  length counter. PCM samples play the way the driver plays them, up to the frame it stops them or
  goes back to their loop. MP2K's voices become instruments with their key splits, drum kits and
  envelopes. Samples keep their own rates and loop points, compressed samples are decoded,
  reversed ones are stored backwards, and fixed-pitch samples play at the mixer's rate on every
  key. MP2K's PSG voices play the Game Boy's square waves, the voice's wave pattern, or noise from
  the Game Boy's LFSR. With `--single-sf2`, each MP2K voice keeps its number as its program, and
  banks tell apart the voices that songs play under the same number, so one SoundFont holds every
  song's voices, even in a game with more voice groups than MIDI has banks. Nintendo R&D2's
  instruments become instruments with their drum kits, key splits and samples for each key, and
  their PSG voices play the Game Boy's waveforms too.

## Known limitations

- With Quintet's driver, the MIDI files keep the keys equal-tempered, although the driver's
  frequency table is out of tune by up to 43 cents in its lowest and highest octaves. A note can
  start up to a frame later than in the game, since the MIDI file keeps it on its own tick.
- A Quintet PCM note whose rate changes during the note, with a slide or the LFO, keeps the loop
  lengths of the rate it started at.
- Quintet's sound effects aren't converted.
- With MP2K, the PSG notes are written at equal-tempered pitch, although the hardware plays high
  notes slightly out of tune, and the driver's reverb is approximated with the reverb send. The
  SoundFonts leave out the PSG's sweep and length, the echo a track can give its notes, and voices
  that a track changes with extended commands, and the conversion leaves out a track's writes to
  the sound registers, with a warning. A note can start up to a frame earlier than in the game,
  since the MIDI file keeps it on its own tick. Without `--voice-channels`, a note that the driver
  cuts off for a later one fades out at its release rate instead.
- With Nintendo R&D2's driver, a note can start up to a frame earlier than in the game, since the
  MIDI file keeps it on its own tick. A track's pitch bend follows its newest note, where the
  driver bends each note on its own. The SoundFonts' envelopes approximate the driver's straight
  lines between points, and the echo, which only the game's code turns on, and the sound effects
  aren't converted.
- A song whose tracks' loops would take more than 8 times as long as the longest to line up
  keeps the longest track's loop, so its other tracks fall out of step each time a player repeats
  it.
- A Quintet noise drum that ends on a steady sound loops its last two frames, which can give a
  long held drum a 30 Hz buzz.
- See "Accuracy" and "Known issues" in the README for the details.
