This release adds support for Ubisoft Milan's sound driver, used by *Tomb Raider: The Prophecy*. It fixes loops that a player looping on the markers repeated differently from the game, and the level of the PSG against the samples in SoundFonts from MP2K and Nintendo R&D2's driver played with FluidSynth. It also fixes the timing of MP2K songs that change the tempo often, which FluidSynth played too fast. supergbamidi converts Game Boy Advance music to MIDI and SoundFont, supporting Konami's, Rare's, Quintet's, Nintendo R&D2's, Brownie Brown's and Ubisoft Milan's drivers, MP2K and Krawall.

Requires 64-bit Windows 10 or later, with no additional runtime DLLs. Drop one or more `.gba`, `.gsflib` or `.minigsf` files on `supergbamidi.exe`, or run it in a terminal; `supergbamidi --help` lists the options. Each file's output goes in a folder next to it.

For macOS and Linux, follow the build instructions in the README. On macOS the build also makes `Supergbamidi.app`, which you can drop files on in the Finder.

## Changes since 1.4.0

- Ubisoft Milan's sound driver, used by *Tomb Raider: The Prophecy*, is supported. The driver plays short sequences whose notes mostly play samples from a kit, one sample for each key, through a software mixer. The sequences can also play the PSG. Each piece of music becomes a MIDI file, with the kit on MIDI channel 10, and a SoundFont with the kit's samples at the mixer's rate. The driver counts time in frames. As with Konami's and Brownie Brown's drivers, the conversion works out the beat from the notes. In the game, a note on a key that the kit has no sample for plays bytes from elsewhere in the cartridge. The conversion leaves such a note out, with a warning. The samples that the game plays on their own, as sound effects, aren't converted.
- A track can keep settings such as its pan or volume when it loops, and a song can keep its tempo, so the first pass through a loop can sound different from the later ones. Where it does, the marked loop now starts at the second pass with Konami's and Nintendo R&D2's drivers and Krawall, as it already did with Quintet's and Brownie Brown's, and with MP2K where the tempo differs. A player that loops on the `loopStart` and `loopEnd` markers then repeats the loop as the game plays it. The files of those songs play the first pass once before the loop, so they're a pass longer.
- MIDI files from Konami's and Brownie Brown's drivers give the tempo again at `loopStart`. The beat that the conversion works out can have another tempo at the loop's end, and a player that keeps its tempo when it jumps back to `loopStart` played the loop's start at that tempo.
- In SoundFonts from MP2K and Nintendo R&D2's driver, the PSG's samples now hold the PSG's level against the samples, and MP2K's samples hold the driver's master volume. The zones' attenuation held these levels before, but FluidSynth applies only 0.4 of a zone's attenuation, so it played the PSG up to 4 dB too loud against the samples. Every SoundFont player now plays them at the levels the GBA mixes them at.
- MIDI files from MP2K now have 960 ticks per quarter note, 40 for each of the driver's 24. FluidSynth rounds its position to a whole tick at each tempo change, and at 24 ticks it played songs that change the tempo often too fast.
- In conversions of *Donkey Kong Country 3*, a note still playing where a MIDI file ends now fades out with the release that its channel set. Before, it took its instrument's release instead.
- In SoundFonts from Nintendo R&D2's driver, a drum now keeps its pan when its track is panned, as in the game. The track's pan used to move it as well.
- With Rare's driver, two tracks that change the tempo within a frame now give the length, the loop and the MIDI file's tempo that the driver plays.
- With Nintendo R&D2's driver, a PSG voice that steps through a table of duties or noise widths now plays the one that the driver holds. The wave voice no longer gets the envelope that the driver leaves out. Levels that two tracks change in one frame now end on the values that the driver plays.
- With MP2K, a track that changes voice twice in one tick now plays each note with its voice. A PSG note with a slow attack that starts just before the end of a song is no longer left out.
- Krawall modules that separate songs with `+++` now convert song by song, in the player's song mode.
- With Konami's driver, a song's loop now starts at the loop points near the one of the track that loops the song. A loop point far before it, or one of a track that ends for good, no longer makes a looping player replay the intro. In the revision of *Yu-Gi-Oh! Dungeon Dice Monsters*, a loop point that starts with a delay now marks the second pass as the loop.
- With `--single-sf2`, the songs of Konami's, Quintet's and Nintendo R&D2's drivers past the 128th, and a song's presets past the 128th, now take free programs in banks that MIDI can select. With Rare's driver, the programs that every set of instruments plays on channel 10 are now in bank 128 too.
- On the command line, several `-t` options now add up, `--tracks` takes Krawall's channels 16 to 19, and Krawall's `--song-count` applies to the table of modules that detection finds. A selection that matches no song no longer makes the output folder, and a file without an extension gets its output in `NAME_output`. A driver whose tables can't be read no longer stops the conversion or `--info`. Both warn and go on with the game's other drivers. `--info` lists each song's warnings, and a mini-GSF's `_lib` tag finds a library whose name differs only in case. Given several mini-GSFs of one set, `--info` lists the set once.
- With `--dump`, each listing has the name of the song's files, and Konami's listings follow each call. Nintendo R&D2's listings add the tracks that `F8` starts. Rare's listings go on past a loop's end that comes before its start. The driver ignores such an end. Quintet's describe `D7 00` and `E7 00 00 00` as the driver plays them.
- The warning that a song was cut off after an hour no longer appears for a song that ends within the hour. A Quintet channel that reads 10000 commands without a note now ends there. Its song used to run for an hour. An error about a file no longer repeats the file's path, Rare's count of the notes that find no slot counts only the chosen tracks, and a Konami note on a sample above `EF` gets a warning that names the sample.
